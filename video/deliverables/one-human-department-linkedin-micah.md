## Primary (~210 words)

I've seen a lot of "zero-human company" content over the past year. It's
inspiring, and it isn't my goal. I don't want to start something I'm not in. I
want more reach inside the domain I've spent my career building.

Thanks to Kimberly Archuleta for having me on to show what that actually looks
like in reserving and rate-making.

The honest problem was never whether AI can do actuarial work. It can. I had
pasted the same validation prompt into Claude something like 30 times —
retyping the guardrails, re-explaining what a loss triangle is, getting a decent
answer, then watching all of it disappear. The real question is how I stop being
the memory.

So we skipped the theory and opened the real workflow:

• A monthly indication project scheduled in the cloud, because when the second
of the month falls on a weekend my laptop is off
• A loss development specialist that builds the triangles and hands off to the
next agent under an explicit contract — the point being that it stops being
just me
• Paperclip vs Claude Cowork, head to head: a control plane that runs zero AI
against a runtime that does the work. Different layers, not competitors.
Approvals go one way, deliverables go the other.

Insurance is one of the only products where the true cost isn't known until
years after the policy is sold. That uncertainty isn't going away. What changes
is how much of my week goes to assembling the file versus judging it.

"One Human Department" — 11 minutes.
